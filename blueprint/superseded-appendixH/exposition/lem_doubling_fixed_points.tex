That the constants are fixed is immediate, each $T(x,\cdot)$ being a probability and
$\lambda$ being $T$-invariant. For the converse, let $f\in L^2(\lambda)$ with
$P_\star f=f$. Jensen's inequality for the probability $T(x,\cdot)$ and the strictly convex
$t\mapsto t^2$ gives $(P_\star f)^2\leq P_\star(f^2)$ at every state, while $\lambda T=\lambda$
gives the two sides the same integral; $\lambda$ being positive at every state, equality holds
everywhere, and equality in Jensen for a strictly convex function forces $f$ to be constant on
each $T(x,\cdot)$, of value $f(x)$. Irreducibility then makes $f$ constant. The same follows
for $P$ because $P_\star=P^*$ and both are contractions
(Lemma~\ref{lem:doubling_operator}\emph{(1)}), and a contraction $Q$ of a Hilbert space has
$\ker(\mathrm{Id}-Q^*)\subseteq\ker(\mathrm{Id}-Q)$: from $Q^*g=g$ come
$\langle Qg,g\rangle=\|g\|^2$ and $\|Qg-g\|^2=\|Qg\|^2-\|g\|^2\leq0$.
