Insert the assumed asymptotic into \eqref{eq:doubling_cut}. The left side is
$\lambda_m(1-\varepsilon(m))=Am^{-p}(1+o(1))$, since $\varepsilon(m)\rightarrow0$. On the
right $\lambda_j\varepsilon(j)=cAj^{-p-1}(1+o(1))$ uniformly for
$\lceil m/2\rceil\leq j\leq m-1$, and there the summand $u\mapsto u^{-p-1}$ is monotone with
consecutive ratio $1+O(1/m)$, so the sum agrees with $\int_{m/2}^{m}u^{-p-1}\,du$ up to a
relative $O(1/m)$; as $p\neq0$ that integral is $\frac{m^{-p}}{p}\left(2^{p}-1\right)$.
Dividing the two sides by $Am^{-p}>0$ leaves \eqref{eq:doubling_cramer}.
