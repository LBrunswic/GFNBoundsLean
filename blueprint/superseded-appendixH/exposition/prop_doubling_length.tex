Lemma~\ref{lem:doubling_supersolution} gives $\mathbb E(\sigma\mid X_0=j)\leq j/(1-c)<+\infty$
and $\sigma<+\infty$ almost surely, and Lemma~\ref{lem:doubling_excursion} then makes the
excursion maximum integrable, being dominated by $\sigma$. At $s=1$ the drift is the constant
$c-1$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:doubling_drift}, so $X_{n\wedge\sigma}+(1-c)(n\wedge\sigma)$ is
a martingale and
$\mathbb E(X_{n\wedge\sigma}\mid X_0=j)=j-(1-c)\,\mathbb E(n\wedge\sigma\mid X_0=j)$. Letting
$n\rightarrow\infty$, the left side tends to $0$ by dominated convergence --- $X_{n\wedge\sigma}
\rightarrow X_\sigma=0$ under the integrable dominating maximum --- and the expectation on the
right tends to $\mathbb E(\sigma\mid X_0=j)$ by monotone convergence. Hence
$\mathbb E(\sigma\mid X_0=j)=j/(1-c)$ exactly, and averaging over the target row gives
$\bar\sigma=\bar\jmath/(1-c)$.
