\emph{One block bounds every index above it.} At a level $\ell\geq m_0$ the descent chain of
Lemma~\ref{lem:doubling_descent} is defined and its item \emph{(3)} gives
$u_m=\mathbb E(Z_\ell\,u_{Y_{N_\ell}}\mid Y_0=m)$ with $Z_\ell>0$, while its item \emph{(2)}
puts $Y_{N_\ell}$ in $[\ell,2\ell)$ almost surely. The extrema of $u$ over that finite block
are therefore attained and strictly positive, and bracket $u_m$ between them times
$\mathbb E(Z_\ell\mid Y_0=m)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:doubling_product} applied at $b:=R_0-1$ and
$H:=c_4$ --- its hypotheses being \eqref{eq:doubling_R0} and $\ell\geq m_0\geq6c_4/\gamma^2$
--- confines that expectation to $[e^{-c_5c_4/\ell},e^{c_5c_4/\ell}]$, which is
\eqref{eq:doubling_block}.

\emph{The rescaled profile is bounded above and below.} Apply the above at $\ell=m_0$ and
$M_1=+\infty$: every $m\geq2m_0$ has $u_m$ within $e^{\pm c_5c_4/m_0}$ of the extrema of $u$
over $[m_0,2m_0)$. Widening those extrema to run over $\{1,\dots,2m_0-1\}$ defines constants
$c_1\leq c_2$, finite and strictly positive because every $\lambda_j$ is, which then bound
$u_m$ at every $m\geq2m_0$ and, the exponentials straddling $1$, at every $m<2m_0$ as well.
Since $u_j=\lambda_j\,j^{p_*}$ this is \eqref{eq:doubling_decay}, and the dependence claimed
for $c_1$ and $c_2$ is that of $m_0$, which rests on $c$ and $d$ alone.
